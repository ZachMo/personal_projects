Election results. The vote counts are the state-level presidential results published by the Instituto Federal Electoral (2000, 2006) and the Instituto Nacional Electoral (2012, 2018, 2024), as tabulated in Spanish Wikipedia's Elecciones federales de México articles. They reconcile with the official national counts. 2018 and 2024 match exactly. The earlier years differ only by the out-of-country vote, which is reported on its own and belongs to no state.

National totals add up the 32 state rows. They leave out the out-of-country vote, which has never been large enough to change a result.

Percentages give each candidate a share of the votes cast for the candidates listed in that election. Null ballots and votes for unregistered candidates stay out of the base, so the five elections compare cleanly. That base also runs a little high next to the figures the press quotes, which divide by every ballot cast. Sheinbaum's 2024 result reads 61.3% here and 59.8% on the wider base. Coalition names are the ones registered for that election, so the same party can turn up in different coalitions from year to year.

Map. State boundaries come from a public-domain Mexico GeoJSON, simplified and drawn on an equirectangular projection standardised at 23°N. The fill shows the winning coalition. A deeper fill means a larger winning share.
