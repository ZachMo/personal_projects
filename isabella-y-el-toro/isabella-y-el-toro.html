A girl lost in Pamplona learns Spanish to help a runaway bull find his way home to the fields.

The story

A fiesta, a rocket, and a bull who wants out

Isabella is twelve. Her family visits Pamplona, Spain, for the Fiesta de San Fermín. Her grandmother grew up there. Mamá has one rule: in Spain, we ask in Spanish.

At noon the festival rocket goes off. The whole city ties on red scarves at once. Then a second bang. A young bull, scared by the noise, breaks out of his pen. The crowd panics, and Isabella loses her family.

The bull finds her in a narrow street. He chases her through the old town. Each time he catches her, she has to use her Spanish to calm him. Slowly she learns to read him. He is not angry. He is scared, tired, hungry and hot.

The twist, in Spanish
«El toro quiere salir.»

"The bull wants to get out." He was never chasing her. He was trying to escape the city. The player has to read this sentence in Spanish to understand the biggest moment of the story. Isabella and the bull escape together, out through the old city gate and home to a field of flowers.

Four acts

From a list of socks to a sentence that opens a gate

Each act asks more of the player's Spanish. Act 1 is single words with English help. By Act 4, even the game's instructions are in Spanish.

1Acto uno · point and click

La villaGet ready for the fiesta

Mamá hands Isabella a list, written only in Spanish. The player searches four rooms for her hat, shoes, sunglasses and more. Decoys sit next to the real items, like socks next to shoes. Clicking anything says its name, so curious players learn extra words. Her little brother, Mateo, holds her backpack hostage behind a three-question quiz, which teaches the battle screen.

2Acto dos · shops

El mercadoShop alone with ten euros

Every stall offers the same menu, so the player has to know which shop sells what. Ask the baker for ice cream and he points you to the right stall, which quietly teaches left and right. Prices are said in words, and the player counts out coins. The fruit seller gives Isabella a free apple, which the bull will need later. The act ends with the crowd counting down in Spanish, the rocket, and Isabella lost.

3Acto tres · chase and maze

¡El toro!Lost, chased, and learning to listen

Isabella runs through the old town and asks strangers for directions. One boy talks far too fast, so the player learns to say "slower, please." The bull catches her three times, and each time an encounter begins. She calms him, feeds him, gives him water in her sun hat, and finally sees what he keeps looking at: the hills beyond the city wall.

¡Libre!Out through the gate and home

Friends from earlier acts come back to help. A drum boy leads a brass band to block the men with ropes. At the old city gate, the player builds four sentences, word by word, to convince the guard to open it. Over the bridge, the first blue in the whole game appears. The bull joins his herd in a field of flowers, the player gives him a name, and Isabella's family finds her.

Game design

Battles where nobody gets hurt

The encounters use RPG Maker's battle screen, but Isabella never attacks. The rule is simple: read the bull, then act. It feels like a game, not a quiz, because the right answer depends on what the player reads.

MIRAR

look — Shows how the bull feels as a Spanish sentence: El toro tiene sed. Free to use.

HABLAR

talk — Pick a phrase. Tranquilo calms him. ¡Vete! does not.

DAR

give — Hand over an item. The apple from the market, the water from the villa, the fan you bought as a treat.

Confianza · trust♥♥♥♥♥Fill five hearts to calm him. Trust carries into the next encounter.
Valor · courage★★★★★A mistake costs a star. At zero, Isabella takes a breath and tries again. No game over.

A wrong choice makes the bull charge, with the warning in Spanish: ¡El toro corre a la izquierda! The player dodges the other way.

How it teaches

Five rules every scene follows

Learn by doing

The player uses a word to make something happen. Nobody just reads a list.

Nobody fails

A wrong answer gets a joke, a hint and another try. Solving it without hints earns a sello (stamp), for players who want a perfect run.

Words come back

The water from Act 1 saves the bull in Act 3. Left and right from the market are needed in the maze.

Text first, audio as a bonus

Every word has a picture, text and a pronunciation guide. The game works fully without sound.

Mi Diccionario

Abuela's old notebook collects every word the player finds. It is a trophy case, and a progress report for parents.

Art direction

The color tells the story

The city lives in yellow, red and black: the July sun, the red festival scarves, the bull. There is no green and no blue until Isabella and the bull leave the city. When the colors open up at the end, players feel the freedom before anyone says it.

Cast

Who Isabella meets

Isabella · 12

The player. Curious, quick and a bit dramatic. Wears her grandmother's red scarf.

El Toro

A young black bull. Scared, not angry. His eyes soften as trust grows. The player names him at the end.

Mateo · 9

Little brother. A pest with a good heart, and the first "battle" of the game.

Señora Carmen

Runs the villa and speaks only Spanish. The player's first real test, and the last line of the game.

The market

Don Julián the baker, Doña Pilar the fruit seller, Chema at the ice cream cart, Señora Lucía with her fans, Nerea with her sunflowers.

The old town

Doña Maite on her balcony, Iker the drum boy, and the old gate guard whose grandfather kept bulls in the countryside.
